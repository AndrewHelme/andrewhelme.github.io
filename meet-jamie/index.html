Why I’m running — again

In 2022 I ran for this seat and said the county needed an independent group to analyze the budget and forecast our financial health, specifically to prevent future property and sewer tax increases.

That was before the reassessment bills went out. Before the emergency special session in Dover. Before the lawsuits, the revised bills, the extended deadline. Before county council raised property taxes 17% this May.

I didn’t win, and none of it got done. I’m not interested in being right about this. I’m interested in fixing it.

What I believe about this job

County council isn’t Washington and it shouldn’t sound like it. Nobody in this district is looking for a culture war. They’re looking for someone who reads the budget, returns the call, shows up at the civic association meeting, and votes a clear yes or no so you know exactly where he stands.

We don’t have Republican taxpayers and Democratic taxpayers here. We have taxpayers. Twelve to one isn’t oversight — and you don’t have to change parties to think one independent set of eyes on the money would be healthy.

“Every dollar the county spends belongs to somebody who earned it. My job is to treat it that way.” — Jamie Collins
