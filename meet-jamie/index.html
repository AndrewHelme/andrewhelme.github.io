District 2 · Brandywine Hundred & Greenville

Meet Jamie

I’m not running against a person. I’m running against a habit — the habit of spending first and explaining never.

Your neighbor

I live here in District 2 — the same stretch of New Castle County I’m asking to represent, from the Pennsylvania line to the Wilmington city line. When the reassessment notices went out, one came to my address too. When the county raised property taxes 17% this May, that vote reached into my household budget the same way it reached into yours.

I’m not a career politician, and I’m younger than most people who run for this seat. I’ve decided to treat both as qualifications. The council has plenty of experience — and that experience delivered a botched reassessment rollout, a $42 million hole, and a 17% tax increase. What the table is missing isn’t another long résumé. It’s somebody with a long stake. I’ll be living with the county’s decisions, and paying its tax bills, for the next fifty years. Nobody currently sitting on that council has more riding on getting this right.

Why I’m running — again

In 2022, I ran for this seat. I lost. My opponent ran a good race, and the voters made their choice — that’s how it should work.

But here is what I said back then, printed on the literature I handed to neighbors across this district:

That was before the reassessment bills went out. Before the emergency special session in Dover. Before the lawsuits, the revised bills, the deadline pushed to New Year’s Eve. Before the council raised county property taxes 17% this May — a vote even one of its own Democrats called part of a “charade” that has to stop.

I’m not interested in being right about this. Being right and losing got this district nothing. I’m interested in fixing it — and this time, the case doesn’t rest on my prediction. It rests on your tax bill.

What I believe about this job

County council isn’t Washington, and it shouldn’t sound like it. Nobody in this district is looking for a culture war. They’re looking for someone who reads the budget before voting on it, returns the phone call, shows up at the civic association meeting, and casts a clear yes or no so you always know where he stands.

We don’t have Republican taxpayers and Democratic taxpayers here. We have taxpayers. Twelve to one isn’t oversight — and you don’t have to change parties to think one independent set of eyes on the money would be healthy.

You may not agree with everything on this website. Good. I’m not asking for your agreement on everything — I’m asking for oversight. Those are different jobs, and the second one is the one on the ballot.

How I’ll operate

Some commitments don’t need a plan document. They need a habit. Mine:

  • Every vote explained. I’ll publish how I voted and why, in plain English, after every council meeting — including the votes you’ll disagree with.
  • Clear yes or no. You’ll never have to guess where I stood.
  • Show up. Civic association meetings aren’t a campaign-season courtesy. They’re where this district actually talks to itself, and I’ll be in the room after the election, not just before it.
  • A returned call. Constituent service is the job. If the county owes you an answer — about a permit, an assessment, a sewer bill — my office chases it until you have one.
“Every dollar the county spends belongs to somebody who earned it. My job is to treat it that way.” Jamie Collins
